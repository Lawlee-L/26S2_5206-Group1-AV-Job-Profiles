## Discussion

### Company Data Collection and ATS Review

- The team reviewed the existing company research table and discussed which company career websites could be supported.
- Each team member should recheck the companies assigned to them.
- Identifying an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) alone is insufficient. The team must also confirm whether the ATS provides an accessible API endpoint.
- Companies using common ATS platforms such as Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby are expected to be easier to support.
- Companies without accessible endpoints, or those requiring complex HTML parsing, may need further investigation and additional development.
- The existing company research table will continue to be used instead of creating a new table.
- Team members should complete the endpoint column wherever possible.
- Companies without confirmed endpoints will be included in a list for further discussion with the client.

### Response to the Client

- Sunjol will prepare a response to the client after the company list has been checked.
- The email should identify companies that may be difficult or impractical to support.
- The complete internal research table does not need to be sent to the client.
- The client should be asked to clarify the location-based use case.
- The client should also clarify whether the reference to a two-week period is a strict requirement or only an example.
- Based on the team’s findings, the client can decide whether difficult companies should be removed from the project scope.

### MVP Scope and Location Filtering

- Location filtering is not required for the initial MVP.
- A location field may be added later if location information is available in the collected job data.
- Filtering jobs by city is technically possible when reliable location data is provided.
- The robotics-related use case appears to be an additional requirement rather than part of the original MVP.
- Supporting searches for robotics-company jobs within a specified city may require both location data and industry classification.
- The team will prioritise the core MVP before considering this additional functionality.

### Data Storage and Sharing

- Nyx has collected data from approximately 16 companies and obtained around 1,400 job records.
- Nyx’s scraper stores the results in DuckDB and includes information such as job location.
- The team discussed whether DuckDB, MySQL, or another database would be more suitable for deployment.
- No final database decision was made during the meeting.
- During the experimentation stage, exporting results to CSV would allow all team members to access the data more easily.
- The database choice can be discussed with the facilitator at the next meeting.
- Nyx’s more detailed scraper may be suitable for the later implementation stage.
- Sunjol’s smaller dataset can be used for initial experimentation.

### Skills Extraction

- The next development stage is to extract skills from job descriptions.
- Team members should experiment with the available job data and compare different extraction methods.
- Potential approaches include:
  - Using an LLM API;
  - Running a local language model through a tool such as Ollama;
  - Developing a custom model;
  - Using keyword-based or rule-based algorithms.
- The experiments do not need to produce a complete solution immediately.
- Team members should share successful approaches, limitations, and findings at the next meeting.
- The team aims to establish a clear skills-extraction approach by Week 7.
- From Week 8, the team expects to begin demonstrating the broader implementation approach.
