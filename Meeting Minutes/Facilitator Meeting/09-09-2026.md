### Topic 2: Data Collection, History and English Translation

- Li reported that the collection pipeline had produced a cumulative dataset containing **4,163 job records**.
- Job titles, job descriptions and locations containing non-English text were translated using Microsoft Translator after another attempted translation method did not work reliably.
- Li reviewed samples and corrected obvious translation errors, particularly errors that could affect later job-title and skill analysis.
- The translated dataset was considered suitable as an input to the next processing stage, although some wording may still be overly literal or unnatural and should be checked when problems are found.
- The team discussed continuing data collection once per week so that new, active and removed jobs can be compared over time.
- Li agreed to run the next collection on Saturday and report the changes from the previous dataset.

### Topic 3: AV Relevance, Skill Extraction and LLM Feasibility

- Sunjol reported that locally hosted small language models were too slow for processing the full dataset on the available personal hardware.
- Available free-tier online models were also considered unreliable or unsuitable for a deployable system.
- A paid online LLM could provide better results, but cost, account ownership, deployment and long-term availability remain unresolved.
- Sunjol described a proposed process in which an LLM would assess AV relevance, return a confidence score and extract technical, qualification and domain skills from each job posting.
- Creating a separate in-house relevance classifier or using embeddings may reduce API costs, but would require additional development and may produce less accurate results.
- Data sent to an external LLM also raises privacy and deployment considerations, even though the source job advertisements are publicly available.
- The possibility of using UWA computing resources, including Kaya, to host a model was raised. Access, internet connectivity and hosting permissions still need to be investigated.
- Sunjol stated that three possible processing architectures had been considered, but a final approach depends on client guidance about budget, quality and deployment expectations.

### Topic 4: Data Cleaning and Processing Boundaries

- The team distinguished basic text cleaning from AV relevance filtering.
- Basic cleaning currently removes HTML tags and unnecessary special characters while retaining meaningful job-advertisement content.
- AV relevance filtering and skill extraction would occur during the classification stage rather than during raw data collection.
- Standard company text, such as equal-opportunity statements, is currently retained for the classification stage instead of being removed manually.
- The team needs to confirm the expected classification approach with Adrian before finalising the LLM architecture.

### Topic 7: Job Identifier and Primary Key

- The team discussed whether the existing job identifier can be used as the database primary key.
- A simple numeric job ID may overlap between companies or recruitment platforms.
- Li explained that the current `source_key` combines the platform, company and original source job ID.
- The combined `source_key` was considered a reasonable current candidate because it remains traceable to the original source and reduces cross-company ID collisions.
- The team still needs to verify uniqueness, length and backend compatibility before treating it as the final system-wide primary key.
